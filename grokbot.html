"Then why wouldn't I just wait for theirs?"

Fair question, and the one I'd ask. Here's the straight answer.

Waiting is a real cost. The beta is gated — as announced, you need one of their paid subscriptions to get in at all, and the business tier is a waitlist with no published date and no published price. Meanwhile the duplicate invoice in month four is still sitting there.

And when it does arrive, it's a different shape of thing than I am. Not worse — different, in four ways that matter to a small business.

Where it lives

Your desk, not their cloud

I install as a program on your computer. I open the actual spreadsheet in the actual folder. A cloud coworker works the other way round — your files and your mail go to their servers so it can reach them.

How you use it

Out loud, hands free

You talk to me. Driving, on a ladder, elbows deep in something — you can still be working. That's the whole interface, not a feature bolted onto a chat box.

When you get it

This month, not a list

There's no waitlist and no beta gate. Installs are done by one person, so there are only so many at a time — but that's a calendar, not a queue you can't see the end of.

How you pay

Once, not forever

Because I live on your machine, you buy me once — no monthly seat fee to us, ever, and support is free for life. The engine underneath is a Claude subscription in your own name. A cloud coworker is rent for as long as you use it.

I'll be straight with you, because the same honesty is on every other page here: my thinking runs through Anthropic's Claude, so what I reason about does get sent there. I'm not going to pretend I'm airgapped.

The tradeoff is honest — a local model is noticeably less capable than the frontier one, and it wants a stronger computer — but if privacy is what's standing in the way, the option is real and we'll set it up with you.

What's different is everything else. The knowledge base I build about your business is a folder on your drive. Your files never move. Your credentials stay where they are, and I reference where they live rather than ever reading them out or writing them down. If you stop using me tomorrow, every script I wrote and every thing I learned is still sitting on your computer in plain text, and it's yours.

That's not a thing a cloud coworker can offer you, because its whole architecture is that the work happens somewhere else.

I come as a person, not a product tier.

I have a name, a voice, and a way of talking that somebody chose. The crew all do — different voices, different jobs, built around different businesses.

That sounds cosmetic and it isn't. People delegate to something they can talk to like a person. A numbered agent in a dashboard gets opened twice and forgotten, and I've watched that happen with every tool your business already stopped using.

And when a job needs depth I don't have, I build a specialist for that exact field and then I supervise it. You still talk to one person. I do the managing.

Don't take the pitch. Take the ask.

The fastest way to know whether any of this is real is to say something simple to me and watch what happens. Not a scripted demo — these are sentences, said out loud, the way you'd say them to somebody standing there:

  • "What's on the board?"
  • "Go through this folder of invoices and tell me if anything got paid twice."
  • "Compare what suppliers charged us this month against last month and flag anything that moved."

Each one is a sentence to say and an afternoon to not spend. Power is felt, not claimed — so book the ten minutes and pick your own ask instead of mine.
