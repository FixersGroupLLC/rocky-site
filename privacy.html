Privacy Policy

Last updated: [DATE]
Written plainly on purpose. If anything here is unclear, ask and we'll fix the wording rather than explain it.

The short version

We collect what you type into our booking form. We use it to arrange your session and follow up with you. We don't sell it, share it, or add you to a mailing list. Ask us to delete it and we will.

Separately: once you're a customer, your business files stay on your own computer. We don't hold them, and we can't.

What we collect from this website

If you fill in the booking form: your name, email address, phone number, business name, website if you have one, and whatever you write in the three description fields.

That's all of it. There's nothing collected quietly in the background.

[ANALYTICS — if you ever add any, it gets disclosed here, named, with what it tracks. Right now this page truthfully says "nothing in the background," and that sentence is worth protecting.]

Why we ask for it

The contact details are so we can send you a session link and reach you if something changes. The description fields are so the ten minutes you spend with Chuck is about your actual business instead of a generic demonstration.

Where it goes

Into a file on a computer we control. Not a third-party CRM, not a marketing platform, not a spreadsheet in somebody's cloud.

How long we keep it

Until you ask us to delete it, or twelve months after our last contact, whichever comes first.

Who else sees it

Nobody outside the people who run this. We don't sell contact information, we don't trade it, and we don't hand it to advertisers.

The one exception anybody has to state: if we were legally compelled to produce it, we would have to. We'd tell you unless we were prohibited from telling you.

Asking us to delete it

Email us and say so. No form, no retention offer, no "are you sure." It goes, and we confirm that it's gone.

Once you're a customer

This is the part that actually matters, and it works differently from most software.

Your files stay on your machine

The assistant runs on your computer. It opens your actual files in your actual folders. The knowledge base it builds about your business is a folder on your own drive, in plain text.

We don't have a copy. There's no cloud account holding your business. If our company vanished tomorrow, your files and everything the assistant learned would still be sitting on your hard drive.

What does leave your machine

The thinking happens through Anthropic's Claude. So whatever the assistant needs to reason about is sent to Anthropic, exactly as it would be in any Claude conversation.

We're not going to soften that. If it's categorizing your transactions, those transactions go to Anthropic. There's no version of this where the intelligence runs on your laptop.

What we do about it:

- Your Claude subscription is in your own name, so that relationship and its settings belong to you, not to us. - At install we turn off optional telemetry and third-party error reporting. - The model-improvement setting in your Claude account can't be changed from your computer, so the installer shows you exactly where it is and you set it yourself.

Anthropic's own policy governs what they do with it, and it's theirs to change, so we link to the live version rather than paraphrasing it here.

What we can see

During an install and during support, we may need access to your machine to fix something. That access is arranged with you, for a purpose, and it's not standing.

We don't have a back door into your assistant and we don't monitor your conversations with it.

The leak scan, both ways

Before any assistant is handed over, an automated scan checks that nothing from our machines — no configuration, no keys, no other client's name — went onto yours. On its first ever run it stopped a build over two client names sitting in a code comment, and that got fixed at the source rather than by loosening the check.

It runs in your favor too. Nothing of yours comes back to us.

Changes to this policy

If we change it materially, we'll tell customers directly rather than quietly updating the date at the top.
